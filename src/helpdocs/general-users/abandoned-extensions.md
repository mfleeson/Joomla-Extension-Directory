# Abandoned extensions: checking and reporting

The **Abandoned Extensions** list shows extensions that appear to be **no longer maintained**.

## How an extension gets on the list

Every entry got there the same way:

1. Something suggested the extension had gone quiet - **broken links** on its listing, an
   **update server** that stopped answering, a **long silence**, or a **report** from a member of
   the public.
2. The JED team **wrote to the developer**.
3. **Nobody answered within the grace period** (30 days by default).

Only then can the team mark the extension as abandoned and, separately, decide to show it on the
public list. Being on the list says nothing about whether an extension is **insecure** - that is
what the [Vulnerable Extensions List](vulnerable-extensions-list.md) is for.

## Reading the list

For each extension the list shows:

- the **Extension** name and the **Last known version**;
- the **Developer**;
- when it was **Marked** as abandoned;
- a link to **View the JED listing**, if it is (or was) listed.

Open an entry for the details. If you maintain the extension, or would like to take it over,
please get in touch with the JED team - an entry can be removed.

## Reporting an extension you think is abandoned

You need to be **signed in**: a report can lead to a public statement about somebody's product,
so every report must be traceable.

Before reporting, note that an extension with no recent release is **not necessarily abandoned**
- it may simply be finished. What helps the team is evidence that the developer has become
unreachable: support requests that go unanswered, a download that no longer works, a site that
has gone.

1. Choose **Report an abandoned extension** (on the Abandoned Extensions page or in the menu).
2. Fill in **Which extension**:
   - **Extension name** (required) - as it appears in the directory, if it is listed;
   - **Last known version** - if you know it;
   - **Extension or developer URL** - the developer's site or the extension's page;
   - **Developer** - who publishes it, if you know;
   - **Why do you think it is abandoned?** (required) - concrete details help most: unanswered
     support requests with dates, a download link that fails, a domain that has expired.
3. Under **About you**, add your **Organisation** only if you report on behalf of one. Your name
   and e-mail address come from your account and are only visible to the JED team - never on the
   public list.
4. Tick the consent box (required) and choose **Send the report**.

You will see: "Thank you. Your report has reached the JED team. We will look into it and contact
the developer before anything is published."

### Limits

- You can file up to **5 reports in 24 hours**.
- You cannot report the same extension again while your earlier report is still being looked
  into.
- If you see *"Your account is not permitted to file reports"*, the JED team has restricted
  reporting on your account.
